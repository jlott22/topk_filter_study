RESULT

Candidate-set size alone does not explain computation time. Performance
degradation is more strongly size-dependent, but its sensitivity to K still
varies by allocator and mission.

IMPORTANT DESIGN LIMITATION

CLIPS and retained CV do not contain any exact shared candidate sizes:

- CLIPS overlapping range: K = {4, 11, 18, 36}
- CV overlapping range: K = {5, 13, 25, 38}

K=1 is shared but is an unreliable boundary condition. I therefore treated K
as a continuous predictor over the overlapping range and used the near matches
4/5, 11/13, and 36/38 only as sensitivity comparisons.

For computation, the response was log mean combined time per allocator call.
For performance, I used paired percentage change in maximum steps from each
algorithm's own R0 baseline; raw maximum steps are not directly comparable
between two different mission objectives.

The mixed models included random intercepts for scenario and
scenario-algorithm combinations.

COMPUTE TIME

All relevant effects were strongly significant:

- Candidate size: p < 1e-300
- Allocator: p < 1e-300
- Mission: p = 3.1e-9
- Candidate size x allocator: p = 3.8e-45
- Candidate size x mission: p < 1e-300
- Mission x allocator: p = 1.9e-169

Adding mission and allocator to the size-only model increased fixed-effect
explanatory power from approximately 1% to 33%. This is strong evidence that
computation is not determined solely by the number of retained candidates.

The near-K comparisons reinforce this result: 13 of 18 allocator-specific
CLIPS-CV comparisons remained significant after Holm correction. The direction
also depended on the allocator. For example, near K=36/38:

- CBAA: CLIPS 1.37 ms versus CV 0.55 ms.
- HIPC: CLIPS 2.20 ms versus CV 9.22 ms.
- DGA: CLIPS 106.5 ms versus CV 374.9 ms.
- ACBBA: 2.54 versus 2.16 ms, not significantly different.

Thus, even approximately equal candidate limits can produce very different
execution times, and the direction of the difference is allocator-specific.

PERFORMANCE

For maximum-step degradation:

- Candidate size was significant: p = 2.2e-24.
- The overall allocator main effect was not significant after controlling for
  size and mission: p = 0.100.
- The overall mission main effect was not significant: p = 0.065.
- Candidate size x allocator was significant: p = 1.1e-7.
- Candidate size x mission was significant: p = 8.0e-4.
- Mission x allocator was not significant: p = 0.908.

None of the 18 individual near-K performance comparisons survived Holm
correction. Many trials had exactly zero change from R0, while a smaller number
had large degradations, producing zero-inflated, highly variable distributions.

The appropriate interpretation is therefore:

- Candidate size is the main systematic driver of maximum-step degradation.
- Allocators do not exhibit a strong constant performance offset after
  controlling for K.
- They do exhibit different degradation slopes as K decreases.
- Therefore, performance is not purely candidate-size-dependent; allocator
  dependence appears through sensitivity to restriction rather than through a
  universal ranking shift.

DISCUSSION-READY CONCLUSION

Within the overlapping low-K range, candidate-set size significantly affected
both computation and maximum-step performance; however, candidate count alone
did not explain the observed behavior. For computation, adding mission and
allocator identity increased fixed-effect explanatory power from approximately
1% to 33%, and significant candidate-size-by-allocator and
mission-by-allocator interactions showed that similarly sized candidate sets
incurred different costs across allocator implementations. Maximum-step
degradation was more directly associated with candidate-set size, although the
significant candidate-size-by-allocator interaction indicated that allocators
differed in their sensitivity to increasingly restrictive candidate limits.
Thus, computation was strongly allocator- and mission-dependent, whereas
performance was primarily size-dependent but modulated by allocator design.

For a definitive same-K claim, both missions should be rerun on an identical
grid such as K = {5, 13, 25, 38}. The present analysis supports an adjusted
overlapping-range comparison, not an exact matched-K experiment.
