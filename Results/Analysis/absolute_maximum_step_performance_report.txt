ABSOLUTE MAXIMUM-STEP PERFORMANCE REPORT

Lower maximum robot steps is better. This metric behaves like a mission
makespan: it records the workload of the most heavily used robot, not total
team effort or computation time.

The primary comparison below uses completed AGX Orin simulation trials:

- CLIPS: R0-R7, generally 496-500 valid trials per condition.
- CV: R0-R4 only, 100 trials per condition.
- HIL results are excluded from ranking because cap-stopped and incomplete
  conditions would make the comparison uneven.

Inter-algorithm tests matched algorithms by trial ID. Friedman tests assessed
overall differences; pairwise Wilcoxon signed-rank tests used Holm correction
across the 15 algorithm pairs.

Key findings:

- CBAA, ACBBA, and DMCHBA form a very tight leading group. Depending on the
  restriction mode, their mean differences are usually less than one step.
- The lowest mean alternates among algorithms:
  - ACBBA: R0, R1, and R5.
  - DMCHBA: R2, R3, and R6.
  - CBAA: R4 and R7.
- DGA has the highest mean in six of eight modes. Its largest baseline
  disadvantage is approximately 4.5 steps relative to ACBBA at R0.
- HIPC is competitive through R6 but degrades sharply at R7, reaching
  approximately 50.5 steps versus 44.3 for CBAA.
- Trial-to-trial variation is extremely large: SDs are approximately 28-38
  steps, much larger than most mean differences between algorithms.
- All mode-level omnibus tests were statistically significant, but the
  separation was small: Kendall's W ranged from 0.008 to 0.042. Thus, the
  large sample detects reliable differences, but algorithm identity produces
  relatively little rank separation compared with scenario-to-scenario
  variability.

INTER-ALGORITHM STATISTICAL INTERPRETATION

CLIPS:

- The mode-averaged omnibus result was significant: chi-square(5) = 115.12,
  p = 3.39e-23, but Kendall's W = 0.047. The practical separation is
  therefore modest.
- CBAA, ACBBA, and DMCHBA were statistically indistinguishable from one
  another after Holm correction.
- CBAA and ACBBA each required approximately 1.3-1.4 fewer steps than PI and
  approximately 0.9-1.0 fewer than HIPC.
- DMCHBA outperformed PI by approximately 1.28 steps but was not
  distinguishable from HIPC.
- PI and HIPC were not distinguishable.
- Every other algorithm outperformed DGA in the mode-averaged comparison.
- Many paired median differences were zero, reinforcing that the significant
  results arise from relatively small shifts across a large number of trials
  rather than large universal differences.

CV:

- The mode-averaged omnibus result was chi-square(5) = 220.73,
  p = 1.04e-45, with W = 0.441. The algorithm ordering is therefore much
  more pronounced.
- HIPC and CBAA were statistically indistinguishable overall: 44.01 versus
  44.24 steps.
- HIPC outperformed ACBBA by 1.36 steps and DMCHBA by 1.98 steps.
- CBAA did not differ significantly from ACBBA, but it outperformed DMCHBA
  by 1.74 steps.
- ACBBA and DMCHBA were not distinguishable.
- PI and DGA were statistically indistinguishable from each other.
- Each of HIPC, CBAA, ACBBA, and DMCHBA significantly outperformed both PI
  and DGA.

DISCUSSION INTERPRETATION

The most defensible paper-level conclusion is not that one allocator
universally dominates. Instead:

- In CLIPS, absolute maximum-step performance is broadly similar across CBAA,
  ACBBA, DMCHBA, HIPC, and PI. DGA is usually somewhat worse, and HIPC becomes
  vulnerable only under the most aggressive R7 restriction.
- In CV, algorithm choice matters more. HIPC performs best when restriction
  is mild, whereas CBAA is most robust as restriction becomes stronger. PI
  and DGA require substantially more steps across the retained range.
- The ranking changes with restriction mode, particularly between HIPC and
  CBAA in CV. This supports treating candidate restriction as an
  algorithm-dependent behavioral intervention rather than assuming a fixed
  global algorithm hierarchy.
- The relative rankings contextualize the behavioral analysis, but they
  should not replace it: maximum steps ignores total team movement,
  communication, computational cost, reliability, and hardware feasibility.

Discussion-ready formulation:

Absolute maximum-step performance depended on both mission structure and
allocation algorithm. In CLIPS, inter-algorithm differences were statistically
detectable but small relative to trial-to-trial variability, with CBAA, ACBBA,
and DMCHBA forming a closely matched leading group and DGA generally requiring
more steps. In CV, algorithm separation was substantially stronger: HIPC
achieved the lowest maximum-step counts under mild restriction, while CBAA
remained invariant across the retained restriction range and became the
best-performing method at stronger restriction levels. PI and DGA consistently
required the greatest maximum robot workload. These results indicate that
value-ranked candidate restriction does not preserve a universal algorithm
ranking; instead, its behavioral effects interact with both mission structure
and allocator design.
